OUR METHOD

How we eliminate guesswork

Most claims about movement fall over because nobody wrote down the starting point. Five rules keep ours standing up.

1

Baseline before we begin

Every programme opens with a recorded starting point, so change can be attributed rather than assumed. Without it, any improvement is a story, not a result.

2

The same measures every time

Consistent instruments, applied the same way across cohorts and sites, so results can be compared rather than just collected.

3

We report what did not move

Measures that showed no change are published alongside those that did. Selective reporting is the fastest way to make a real result worthless.

4

The method travels with the number

Source, date range, sample and calculation are recorded before anything is published, and we can produce them on request.

5

Open to outside scrutiny

Our pilot data and methodology are available for independent review. Findings nobody else is allowed to check are not findings.

6

Consent captured up front

Participants, and the parents of any child taking part, agree to how their data may be used before it is collected, including any later use in research or reporting.

The long game

Movement as prevention, not repair

Physical activity is associated with reduced risk of cardiovascular disease, type 2 diabetes and several cancers, and with reduced symptoms of depression and anxiety. The World Health Organization sets this out across the lifespan in its physical activity and sedentary behaviour guidelines. That evidence is not what is missing.

What is actually missing

Delivery. Structured, repeatable, measured movement in the places people already are — schools, workplaces, after-school care and community settings — run by people whose job it is to turn up every week and record what happened.

That is the gap Everest works in. We are building the delivery capability and the measurement discipline to sit alongside health and education systems, not to duplicate them.

What we are not

Everest does not diagnose, does not treat, and does not claim that any programme we run prevents illness in an individual. We are a delivery and measurement organisation, and we work within our scope of practice.

What we can do is deliver movement consistently, measure it properly, and contribute evidence about what works, for whom, and at what cost per participant.

What our first pilot can and cannot show

The EMPOWER pilot is a feasibility and implementation study. With 75 children over six weeks at a single partner and no control group, it is designed to test whether the programme can be delivered consistently, whether children engage with it, and whether the measures we have chosen hold up in the field. It is not designed to demonstrate disease prevention and we will not present it as though it were. Establishing that takes larger cohorts, longer follow-up and independent design — which is the path this pilot is the first step on.

Evidence position based on the World Health Organization guidelines on physical activity and sedentary behaviour. Everest programmes are delivered by qualified trainers and coaches and are not a substitute for medical care.

How we handle evidence

Credibility matters more than big numbers. We hold ourselves to a clear evidence hierarchy, strongest evidence first, and never publish unverified or inflated figures.

1

Verified quantitative outcomes

Participation, adherence, retention, strength and movement change, program completion and validated survey results.

2

Case studies

A defined starting point, intervention, duration and measured outcome.

3

Testimonials

Shared with full permission and appropriate context.

4

Partner feedback

Feedback from partners and repeat engagements.

5

Program methodology

Program descriptions and delivery methodology.

6

Community & media

Community images, media coverage and milestone records.
