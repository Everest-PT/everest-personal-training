The problem with most meal prep is not the cooking. It is that by Wednesday you cannot face what you made on Sunday. This one holds up, because the sauce keeps the chicken from drying out and the spice does not fade in the fridge.

Five servings, roughly **52g of protein** each, about 35 minutes start to finish.

## Why this one works for training

**Chicken breast** provides a strong lean protein source to support muscle repair and recovery. At 750g across five servings you are getting a serious protein dose per box without needing a shake to top it up.

**Rice** provides the carbohydrate to replenish glycogen and fuel your training. This is the part people cut first and regret later — if you train hard and eat no carbohydrate, the sessions go backwards before the scale does anything interesting.

**Greek yoghurt** does double duty. It boosts the protein further and creates a creamy texture without needing cream, which is where a lot of curries quietly pick up their fat content.

**The vegetables** add fibre, volume and micronutrients, which support digestion and fullness. Volume matters more than people expect: a bigger meal at the same calories is a meal you are less likely to follow with something else.

## A note on the macros

The figures above are approximate, calculated per serving across five containers. Change the rice quantity, swap breast for thigh, or add oil to the pan and they move. Use them as a solid estimate rather than a laboratory measurement.

Prep once. Eat well. Train hard. Most people do not need a better diet so much as a fridge with the right thing already in it.
